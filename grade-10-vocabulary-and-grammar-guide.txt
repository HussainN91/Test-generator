### II. Grammar: Present Simple and Continuous

#### 1. Present Simple
*   **Forms**:
    *   *Positive*: 
        *   `I / You / We / They` + verb (e.g., *get, study, watch, go*)
        *   `He / She / It` + verb-s/-es/-ies (e.g., *gets, studies, watches, goes*)
    *   *Negative*: `don't` / `doesn't` + infinitive (e.g., *I don't study*, *He doesn't study*)
    *   *Questions*: `Do` / `Does` + subject + infinitive (e.g., *Do you go?*, *Does he go?*)
*   **Usage**:
    *   **Regular habits/routines**: Things that happen regularly (e.g., *I walk to school every day.*)
    *   **Facts/General truths**: Permanent states (e.g., *We live near my grandparents.*)
    *   **State Verbs**: Verbs describing thoughts or feelings (not normally used in continuous form):
        *   *believe, hate, know, like, love, mean, need, own, prefer, understand, want, remember*
        *   *Example*: *Mike knows the answer.* / *I don't remember your address.*

#### 2. Present Continuous
*   **Forms**:
    *   *Positive*: `am` / `is` / `are` + verb-ing (e.g., *I'm playing*, *She's helping*, *They're working*)
    *   *Negative*: `'m not` / `isn't` / `aren't` + verb-ing
    *   *Questions*: `Am` / `Is` / `Are` + subject + verb-ing (e.g., *Are you working?*)
*   **Usage**:
    *   **Actions happening now**: In progress at the moment of speaking (e.g., *We're doing our homework.*)
    *   **Temporary situations**: Ongoing but not permanent (e.g., *I'm helping my dad in his office this week.*)
    *   **Future plans/arrangements**: Definite planned events (e.g., *They're playing table tennis after school.*)

### II. Grammar: Past Simple (Regular & Irregular)

#### 1. Verb Forms
*   **Regular Verbs**: Verb + `-ed` (e.g., *look* → *looked*). 
    *   *Spelling variation*: Verbs ending in consonant + `-y` change `-y` to `-ied` (e.g., *study* → *studied*).
*   **Irregular Verbs**: Have unique forms that do not end in `-ed`.
    *   *buy* → *bought* | *make* → *made* | *do* → *did* | *meet* → *met* | *feel* → *felt* | *put* → *put* | *get* → *got* | *run* → *ran* | *give* → *gave* | *say* → *said* | *go* → *went* | *take* → *took* | *have* → *had* | *think* → *thought* | *know* → *knew* | *wear* → *wore*
*   **The Verb "Be"**: Has two past forms:
    *   `I / He / She / It` + **was** / **wasn't**
    *   `You / We / They` + **were** / **weren't**
    *   *Questions*: *Was she late?* / *Were you there?*

#### 2. Negatives and Questions (All other verbs)
*   *Negatives*: `didn't` + infinitive (e.g., *They didn't buy it.*)
*   *Questions*: `did` + subject + infinitive (e.g., *Did you find my bag?*)

#### 3. Usage
*   **Completed past actions**: Used to talk about finished actions, feelings, and states in the past.
*   **Time reference**: Often mentions the specific time when things happened (e.g., *yesterday, last night, last Saturday*).

| Word | Type | Definition |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **enough** | adv | As much as is necessary |
| **not enough** | adv | Less than is necessary |
| **too** | adv | Used before adjectives/adverbs to mean ‘more than is allowed, necessary, or possible’ |

### II. Grammar: Comparatives and Superlatives

#### 1. Form Rules
*   **One-syllable adjectives**: Add `-er` (comparative) and `-est` (superlative) (e.g., *new* → *newer* → *the newest*).
*   **Ending in `-e`**: Add `-r` and `-st` (e.g., *nice* → *nicer* → *the nicest*).
*   **One-syllable vowel + consonant**: Double the final consonant (e.g., *big* → *bigger* → *the biggest*).
*   **Two-syllables ending in `-y`**: Change `-y` to `-i` and add `-er` and `-est` (e.g., *funny* → *funnier* → *the funniest*).
*   **Two or more syllables**: Use `more` and `the most` (e.g., *interesting* → *more interesting* → *the most interesting*).
*   **Irregular Adjectives**:
    *   *good* → *better* → *the best*
    *   *bad* → *worse* → *the worst*

#### 2. Sentence Structures
*   **Comparative (+ than)**: Comparing two entities in a positive direction (e.g., *Your laptop is faster than mine.*).
*   **Not as... as**: Saying two entities are not equal (e.g., *My laptop isn't as fast as yours.*).
*   **Superlatives**: Often preceded by `the` to highlight a single outlier in a group (e.g., *This laptop is the fastest in the shop.*).

### II. Grammar: Past Simple and Continuous Combined

#### 1. Grammatical Contrast
*   **Past Continuous**: Describes background actions in progress (e.g., *It was raining.*, *I was texting a friend.*).
*   **Past Simple**: Describes completed, sequential actions or a sudden event (e.g., *I met my friends.*, *She showed me her bag and then I went home.*).

#### 2. Interrupted Actions (When and While)
*   We use the **Past Continuous** for the ongoing background action, and the **Past Simple** for the shorter action that interrupts it.
*   **When**: Often precedes the past simple action.
    *   *Example*: *I was cycling home when suddenly my bike slipped.*
*   **While**: Often precedes the past continuous action.
    *   *Example*: *Tomas emailed me twice while I was mending my bike.*

### II. Grammar: Quantifiers

#### 1. Some and Any
*   Used with **plural countable** and **uncountable** nouns.
    *   **Some**: In positive sentences (e.g., *We bought some petrol and some sweets.*).
    *   **Any**: In questions and negative sentences (e.g., *I haven't got any homework.* / *Are there any good clubs?*).

#### 2. Large Quantities: Much, Many, and A Lot Of
*   **A Lot Of**: Used with both plural countable and uncountable nouns in positive, negative, and question forms (e.g., *I took a lot of photos.*).
*   **Many**: Primarily used with **plural countable** nouns in negative sentences and questions (e.g., *Are many people coming?*).
*   **Much**: Primarily used with **uncountable** nouns in negative sentences and questions (e.g., *Is there much traffic?*).

#### 3. Small Quantities: A Few and A Little
*   Used in **positive sentences and questions** (never in negative structures).
    *   **A Few**: Used with **plural countable** nouns (e.g., *I invited a few friends.*).
    *   **A Little**: Used with **uncountable** nouns (e.g., *I'd like a little sugar, please.*).

### II. Grammar: Have To, Must, and Should

#### 1. Rules, Necessity & Prohibition: Have To vs. Must
*   **Have to**: Changes forms according to subject and tense.
    *   *Present*: `has to` (singular) / `have to` (plural/I/you).
    *   *Past*: `had to` (for all subjects; serves as the past tense of both *have to* and *must*).
    *   *Negative*: `don't / doesn't / didn't have to` means **it is not necessary** (e.g., *We don't have to wear a uniform.*).
    *   *Note*: No contractions are allowed in positive statements (e.g., write *He has to stay*, never *He's to stay*).
*   **Must**: Has only one form and lacks a past tense.
    *   *Prohibition (Mustn't)*: Means **not allowed/forbidden** (e.g., *You mustn't sleep in class.*).

#### 2. Advice & Suggestions: Should
*   **Should / Shouldn't**: Lacks variation. Used to express whether an action is a good or bad idea (e.g., *I should buy a present.*).
*   *Questions*: Used when asking for advice (e.g., *Which shoes should I wear?*).

*   **Structure**: `has / have (not) + past participle`

### II. Grammar: Present Perfect vs. Past Simple | How Long, For, and Since

#### 1. Present Perfect vs. Past Simple
*   **Present Perfect**: Focuses on **life experiences** up to the present without mentioning a specific time. No past-time phrases are allowed.
    *   *Example*: *She's seen that film.*
*   **Past Simple**: Focuses on **specific past times** when actions were completed.
    *   *Example*: *She saw the film last week.*

#### 2. How Long, For, and Since
*   **How long?**: Questions a period of time extending up to the present (e.g., *How long have you lived here?*).
*   **For**: Introduces the **duration/length** of a time period (e.g., *for ten years, for a long time*).
*   **Since**: Introduces the **start point** of an action or state (e.g., *since six o'clock, since last year*).
    *   *Note*: If a verb follows `since`, it must be in the **Past Simple** (e.g., *I have known him since I started school.*).

*   `Unless` is equivalent to **if not** (e.g., *Plants don't grow unless they have light.* = *... if they don't have light.*).

| **Present Continuous** (*"I am running"*) | **Past Continuous** (*he was running*) |
| **Will** (*"I will win"*) | **Would** (*he would win*) |
| **Can** (*"I can sit"*) | **Could** (*they could sit*) |

#### 2. Reporting Verbs: Say vs. Tell
*   **Say**: Does **not** take an indirect object.
    *   *Incorrect*: *Mark said me he went to Italy.*
    *   *Correct*: *Mark said (that) he went to Italy.*
*   **Tell**: **Must** be followed by a direct object/pronoun.
    *   *Correct*: *Mark told me (that) he went to Italy.*

### II. Grammar: Past Simple Passive

#### 1. Form
*   `was / were (not) + past participle`
    *   *Active*: *Two school students found the pictures.*
    *   *Passive*: *The pictures were found by two school students.*

#### 2. Usage
*   Used when **the actor/agent is unknown, obvious, or unimportant** (e.g., *That picture was sold for a thousand pounds.*).
*   We use **by** to introduce the actor only if we need to emphasize who completed the action (e.g., *The story was published by the national newspapers.*).

# Grade 11 Vocabulary and Grammar Reference Guide

This study guide systematically organizes the complete Grade 11 English curriculum. All vocabulary sets, word-building forms, and phrasal verbs are presented chronologically alongside their corresponding grammar units. All bulky practice exercises have been omitted to provide a clean, high-density study reference.

### Word Formation: Prefixes `un-` and `dis-`
*   **disagree** (v): to have a different opinion from someone else
*   **disappear** (v): to become impossible to see or find
*   **dislike** (v): to not like someone or something
*   **unhappy** (adj): sad
*   **uninteresting** (adj): not interesting
*   **unkind** (adj): slightly cruel
*   **unlucky** (adj): having bad things happen to you
*   **unpleasant** (adj): not enjoyable or pleasant
*   **unreliable** (adj): unable to be trusted or depended upon

### Grammar: Countable & Uncountable Nouns; Determiners
#### Countable Nouns
*   Can be singular or plural (e.g., *card → cards*, *phone → phones*).
*   **Irregular Plurals**: *child → children*, *woman → women*, *person → people*.

#### Uncountable Nouns
*   Always singular (e.g., *bread*, *information*, *news*, *homework*, *money*, *water*).
*   Can be measured using counting phrases: *a slice of bread*, *a great deal of money*, *two glasses of water*.

#### Nouns with Dual Usage
*   **noise**: "There’s a strange noise coming from that box" (countable) vs. "Can you hear that noise?" / "Noise is annoying" (uncountable).
*   **hair**: "There’s a hair in my soup!" (countable) vs. "I’ve got long hair" (uncountable).
*   **coffee**: "Do you want a coffee?" (countable cup) vs. "Coffee is my favourite drink" (uncountable substance).

#### Determiners
*   **some, any, no, plenty of, (not) a lot of**: Used with both plural countable and uncountable nouns.
    *   *Positive*: "I've got some biscuits and some cheese." / "There are no oranges." / "There are a lot of people."
    *   *Negative/Questions*: "We haven't got any milk." / "Is there any cake?"
    *   *Offers/Requests*: Use **some** in questions when offering or expecting a "Yes" answer (e.g., "Would you like some tea?").
*   **many**: Used with plural countable nouns in positive, negative, and question forms.
*   **much**: Used with uncountable nouns (primarily in negative sentences and questions).
*   **several**: Used exclusively with plural countable nouns (e.g., "I've got several friends").

### Grammar: -ing Forms (Present Participle / Gerund)
We use the **-ing form** of a verb in three main ways:

#### 1. After Verbs of Liking and Disliking
*   Follows verbs like: `like`, `love`, `enjoy`, `dislike`, `hate`, `not mind`, `can't stand`.
    *   *Example*: I love going out with my friends. My mum can't stand doing housework.

#### 2. After Adjective + Preposition Phrases
*   Follows adjective phrases such as: `good at`, `bad at`, `keen on`, `interested in`, `crazy about`, `afraid of`, `tired of`.
    *   *Example*: My brother is good at singing. Our cats are afraid of getting wet.

#### 3. As Part of the Sentence Subject
*   Placed at the beginning of a clause as a gerund subject.
    *   *Example*: Playing online games is a fun thing to do. Keeping in touch with friends is easy.

## Unit 3: Sports & Present Simple vs. Continuous

### Vocabulary: Sports Phrases
| Sports Phrase | Definition |
| :--- | :--- |
| **beat the other team** | to defeat or do better than someone in a competition |
| **enter a competition or tournament** | to take part in a competition or tournament |
| **give (someone) the chance** | to provide someone with an opportunity to do something |
| **have a go at something** | to try something you have not done before |
| **join a club** | to become a member of a club |
| **lose a game or match** | if you lose a game or competition, the opposing team or person wins |
| **miss an opportunity to do something** | to not use an opportunity to do something |
| **score a goal or point** | to get points in a game or test |
| **win a prize, medal, game or match** | to get the most points, or to get a prize, in a competition or game |

### Word Formation: Suffixes `-ist`, `-er`, `-or`
These suffixes are added to base words to indicate a person performing an action or sport:
*   **-ist**: `canoeist` (travels in a canoe)
*   **-er**: `climber` (climbs mountains), `footballer` (plays football), `runner` (runs), `sailor` (sails boats), `surfer` (surfs), `swimmer` (swims), `winner` (wins)
*   **-or**: `competitor` (tries to win a competition), `spectator` (watches an event/sport)

### Grammar: Present Simple vs. Present Continuous
#### Present Simple
*   **Form**: 
    *   *Positive*: `I/You/We/They play/study` \| `He/She/It plays/studies`
    *   *Negative*: `I/You/We/They don't play/study` \| `He/She/It doesn't play/study`
    *   *Questions*: `Do I/you/we/they play?` \| `Does he/she/it play?`
*   **Usage**:
    *   Regular habits, routines, or general truths (*"Most evenings, we play video games after dinner."*).
    *   Facts, systems, and scientific processes (*"Water freezes when it gets cold."*).
    *   **State Verbs**: Verbs describing feelings, thoughts, or states that are not normally used in continuous forms: `believe`, `dislike`, `hate`, `know`, `like`, `love`, `prefer`, `remember`, `think`, `understand`, `want`, `wish`.

#### Present Continuous
*   **Form**: `am/is/are + verb-ing`
    *   *Positive*: `I'm watching` \| `He's running` \| `They're sitting`
    *   *Negative*: `I'm not watching` \| `He isn't running` \| `They aren't sitting`
    *   *Questions*: `Am I watching?` \| `Is he running?` \| `Are they sitting?`
*   **Usage**:
    *   Actions happening exactly at the moment of speaking (*"We're sitting on the train at the moment."*).
    *   Definite future plans and confirmed arrangements (*"I'm meeting some friends after school."*).

## Unit 4: Extreme Weather & Past Simple vs. Used To

### Vocabulary: Extreme Weather
| Vocabulary Word | Part of Speech | Definition |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **blow** | v | (wind) to move and make currents of air |
| **earthquake** | n | a sudden movement of the Earth’s surface, often causing severe damage |
| **fall down** | v | to fall onto the ground |
| **flood** | n | a situation in which water covers an area that is usually dry |
| **flow** | v | (liquid) to move somewhere in a smooth, continuous way |
| **lightning** | n | a sudden flash of light in the sky during a storm |
| **pour** | v | to rain heavily |
| **rise** | v | to increase in level |
| **shake** | v | to make quick, short movements from side to side or up and down |
| **snowstorm** | n | a storm with heavy snowfall |
| **tornado** | n | an extremely strong wind blowing in a circle that destroys buildings |

### Phrasal Verbs
*   **blow away**: to be moved or make something move on a current of air
*   **burn down**: to destroy something (especially a building) completely by fire
*   **clear up**: (weather) the clouds and rain disappear, revealing clear skies
*   **come out**: (sun/moon/stars) to appear in the sky
*   **fall down**: to fall to the ground
*   **put out**: to extinguish or stop something burning

### Grammar: Past Simple and Used To
#### Past Simple
*   **Form**: Regular verbs add `-ed` (*open → opened*). Irregular verbs change completely (*break → broke*, *run → ran*, *think → thought*, *rise → rose*, *put → put*).
*   **Negatives & Questions**: Formed using `did` / `didn't` + base infinitive.
    *   *Example*: I didn't open the window. Did she lock the car?
*   **Verb "to be"**: has two forms: `was/wasn't` (singular) and `were/weren't` (plural).
*   **Usage**:
    *   Single completed actions in the past.
    *   Events that occurred at a specific, defined time.
    *   Regular past habits or repetitive past actions.

#### Used To
*   **Form**: `used to + infinitive` (Positive) \| `didn't use to + infinitive` (Negative) \| `Did you use to + infinitive?` (Question)
*   **Usage**: To describe habits, states, or situations in the past that are no longer true in the present.
    *   *Example*: We used to live in a small flat. (We don’t live there now).

## Unit 5: Verbs for Making Things & Past Continuous Interruption

### Vocabulary: Verbs for Making Things
| Vocabulary Word | Part of Speech | Definition |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **create** | v | to make something happen or exist |
| **customise** | v | to change something to make it suitable for a particular person or purpose |
| **decorate** | v | to make something look more attractive by putting things on or around it |
| **design** | v | to draw or plan something before making it |
| **fix** | v | to repair something |
| **mend** | v | to repair something that is broken, torn, or not working correctly |
| **rebuild** | v | to build something again after it has been damaged |
| **recycle** | v | to put used paper, glass, plastic, etc. through a process so it can be used again |
| **sew** | v | to join things together with a needle and thread |

### Grammar: Past Simple and Past Continuous
#### Past Continuous
*   **Form**: `was/were + present participle (verb-ing)`
    *   *Positive*: I was eating / You were sleeping
    *   *Negative*: I wasn't eating / You weren't sleeping
    *   *Questions*: Was I eating? / Were you sleeping?
*   **Usage**:
    *   To talk about actions and situations that were actively in progress at a specific moment in the past (*"It was raining all night."*).
    *   To describe background atmosphere or settings within a story (*"We were travelling with some friends..."*).
    *   **Interrupted Actions**: To show an action in progress (Past Continuous) that is interrupted by a sudden, shorter action (Past Simple).
        *   *Example*: I **was waiting** for a bus when you **texted**.
*   **State Verbs Restriction**: State verbs are not used in the past continuous (e.g., use *"I didn't understand it"*, never *"I wasn't understanding it"*).

## Unit 6: Health Verbs & Modals (1)

### Vocabulary: Health Verbs
| Vocabulary Word | Part of Speech | Definition |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **ache** | v | to feel continuous dull pain |
| **beat** | v | (heart) to make regular, rhythmic movements and sounds |
| **bleed** | v | to have blood flowing out from your body |
| **blink** | v | to open and close your eyes very quickly |
| **blow your nose** | pv | to clear your nasal passage by forcing air through it |
| **breathe** | v | to take air into and out of your lungs |
| **burn** | v | to be hurt or damaged by fire or heat |
| **cough** | v | to force air out of your throat with a sudden, sharp sound |
| **cut** | v | to injure yourself on a sharp object, causing bleeding |
| **injure** | v | to hurt a physical part of your body |
| **recover** | v | to become healthy, fit, or happy again after an illness or injury |
| **yawn** | v | to take a deep breath with your mouth wide open because you are tired or bored |

### Grammar: Modals of Obligation, Necessity, and Advice (1)
#### 1. must, should, ought to
*   These modals are helper verbs. Their forms do not change with the subject, and they are always followed by the base infinitive of the main verb (without *to*).
    *   *Positive*: `must` (strong obligation) \| `should` / `ought to` (advice)
    *   *Negative*: `mustn't` (prohibition) \| `shouldn't` (negative advice)

#### 2. have to / has to
*   Unlike other modal verbs, **have to** changes its form depending on the subject tenses and persons.
    *   *Positive*: `I/You/We/They have to` \| `He/She/It has to`
    *   *Negative*: `I/You/We/They don't have to` \| `He/She/It doesn't have to`
    *   *Questions*: `Do you have to?` \| `Does he have to?`

#### Modal Meanings & Comparisons
| Modal | Meaning | Strength / Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **must** \| **have to** | Absolute necessity / strong obligation | Rules, laws, or urgent requirements (*"You must rest your leg."*). |
| **mustn't** | Prohibition / extremely important not to | Against the rules (*"You mustn't use your phone during the exam."*). |
| **don't have to** | Lack of necessity | It is not required (*"My mother doesn't have to work at weekends."*). |
| **should** \| **ought to** | Positive advice | It is a good idea (*"You should try to relax before your exam."*). |
| **shouldn't** | Negative advice | It is a bad idea (*"You shouldn't eat sweets before a meal."*). |

### Word Families
*   **achieve** (v) / **achievement** (n)
*   **advertise** (v) / **advertisement** (n)
*   **entertain** (v) / **entertainer** (n) / **entertaining** (adj) / **entertainment** (n)
*   **music** (n) / **musical** (adj) / **musician** (n)
*   **perform** (v) / **performance** (n) / **performer** (n)
*   **record** (n) / **record** (v)

### Grammar: Present Perfect and Past Simple
#### Present Perfect
*   **Form**: `has/have + past participle`
*   **Usage**: To describe an action that started in the past but has a direct link or consequence in the present.
*   **Key Time Adverbs**:
    *   **just**: Explains something that finished a few moments ago. Placed after *have/has* and before the main verb (*"I've just arrived"*).
    *   **already**: Explains that an action occurred sooner than expected. Placed after *have/has* or at the end of positive clauses (*"I've already made one recording"*).
    *   **yet**: Used in negative statements and questions to mean "up to this point". Placed at the very end of the clause (*"I haven't finished yet"*).
    *   **ever**: Used in questions to mean "at any time in your life up to now". Placed before the main verb (*"Have you ever made a recording?"*).

#### Past Simple vs. Present Perfect Comparison
*   **Past Simple**: Used for completed actions that started and finished at a defined time in the past (*"We spent hours in the recording studio yesterday."*).
*   **Present Perfect**: Used when the exact time of the action is unspecified or irrelevant, focusing instead on life experience or current results (*"We've spent hours in the recording studio."*).

## Unit 8: Architecture & Comparative/Superlative Adjectives

### Vocabulary: Amazing Architecture
| Vocabulary Word | Part of Speech | Definition |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **brand new** | adj | completely, 100% new |
| **classic** | adj | typical, traditional, or serving as an outstanding representative |
| **cosy** | adj | comfortable, warm, and inviting |
| **fresh** | adj | new, clean, or different from what was previously there |
| **historic** | adj | important in history or likely to be important in future history |
| **modern** | adj | relating to the current or present time rather than the past |
| **original** | adj | special, unique, and interesting because of not being a copy |
| **spectacular** | adj | extremely beautiful, exciting, or surprising |
| **traditional** | adj | following long-established customs or cultural habits |
| **unusual** | adj | different, unexpected, and not ordinary |

### Strong Adjectives and Adverbs
Adverbs of degree can modify regular or strong adjectives:
*   **absolutely** (adv): used exclusively with *strong adjectives* to mean "completely" or "very" (e.g., *absolutely ancient*, *absolutely freezing*).
*   **extremely / incredibly** (adv): used with *regular adjectives* (e.g., *extremely cold*, *incredibly tired*).

#### Base vs. Strong Adjective Pairs
*   Old → **ancient**
*   New → **brand new**
*   Large → **enormous**
*   Tired → **exhausted**
*   Cold → **freezing**
*   Good/Exciting → **spectacular**
*   Bad/Unpleasant → **terrible**

### Grammar: Comparative and Superlative Adjectives
#### Adjective Spelling Rules
1.  **One-syllable**: Add `-er` and `-est` (*new → newer → newest*).
2.  **Ending in 'e'**: Add `-r` and `-st` (*nice → nicer → nicest*).
3.  **Vowel + Consonant**: Double the final consonant (*big → bigger → biggest*).
4.  **Ending in '-y'**: Change `-y` to `-ier` and `-iest` (*tidy → tidier → tidiest*).
5.  **Multi-syllable**: Use `more` and `the most` (*active → more active → the most active*).
6.  **Irregular Adjectives**:
    *   *good → better → the best*
    *   *bad → worse → the worst*
    *   *far → further/farther → the furthest/farthest*
7.  **Compound Adjectives**: Use `more` and `the most` (*user-friendly → more user-friendly*).

#### Comparative Structures
*   **Superiority**: `comparative adjective + than` (*"Your bike is newer than mine."*)
*   **Inferiority**:
    *   `less + adjective + than` (*"The dictionary was less useful than the grammar book."*)
    *   `not as + adjective + as` (*"My bike isn't as good as yours."*)
*   **Equality**: `as + adjective + as` (*"My bedroom is as big as my brother's."*)

#### Superlative Structures
*   Identifies one item as containing the maximum or minimum of a quality among a group: `the + superlative` or `possessive + superlative`.
    *   *Example*: Rob is the fastest runner. Rob is the school's most successful runner.

*   *Note*: Degree modifiers such as **enough**, **too**, **very**, and **not... enough** are used to change adjective intensity (e.g., *too expensive*, *not fast enough*).

### Grammar: Future Forms and Future Continuous
#### 1. Present Continuous for Plans
*   Used to discuss highly definite, pre-arranged future schedules or appointments.
    *   *Example*: Charlie is leaving on Tuesday. I'm meeting my doctor at 4:00 PM.

#### 2. Will
*   Used for:
    *   General or subjective future predictions (*"The price of phones will fall."*).
    *   Spontaneous offers or promises (*"I'll help you set up the equipment."*).

#### 3. Going To
*   Used for:
    *   Personal intentions, plans, and decisions (*"We're going to drive to the coast."*).
    *   Predictions based on current, clear physical evidence (*"Look at those clouds! It is going to rain."*).

#### 4. Future Continuous
*   **Form**: `will/won't + be + verb-ing`
    *   *Positive*: I'll be working \| *Negative*: He won't be playing \| *Question*: Will you be living...?
*   **Usage**: To talk about actions that are certain to be actively in progress at a specific, defined point in the future.
    *   *Example*: This time next year, I'll be studying at university.

| **population** | n | the total number of individuals of a species living in an area |

### Fixed Prepositional Phrases: `at`
*   **at first**: at the beginning of a situation
*   **at its best**: at the absolute peak of quality or achievement
*   **at least**: as much as or more than a specified amount
*   **at long last**: finally, after a long delay
*   **at once**: simultaneously or immediately
*   **at present**: currently, right now
*   **at risk**: in a dangerous situation where harm is likely

### Grammar: Conditional Sentences & Unless
#### Zero Conditional
*   **Form**: `If/When + Present Simple, Present Simple`
*   **Usage**: To state general truths, scientific facts, or automatic habits.
    *   *Example*: If you heat ice, it melts. A lioness is dangerous if she has cubs.

#### First Conditional
*   **Form**: `If + Present Simple, will + base verb`
*   **Usage**: To describe highly likely, realistic future possibilities.
    *   *Example*: If the forests disappear, the local weather will change.

#### Second Conditional
*   **Form**: `If + Past Simple, would + base verb`
*   **Usage**: To describe hypothetical, imaginary, or highly improbable situations in the present or future.
    *   *Example*: If I had a garden, I'd grow fruit. (Fact: I don't have a garden).

#### Unless
*   **Meaning**: Acts as a replacement for "if... not" (`unless` = `if not`).
    *   *Example*: We wouldn't eat honey **unless** we liked it. (= if we didn't like it)

| **unpack** | v | to remove contents from a suitcase or box |

### Fixed Prepositional Phrases: `on`
*   **on board**: on an active boat, train, or aircraft
*   **on display**: shown publicly for people to view
*   **on foot**: walking rather than using transport
*   **on my own**: alone, without companion assistance
*   **on purpose**: intentionally, not by accident
*   **on sale**: available for purchase in a shop (often at a lower price)
*   **on time**: punctually, neither early nor late

### Grammar: Modals of Obligation, Necessity, and Advice (2)
#### Advice
*   **should / shouldn't**: Used to express personal recommendations, opinions, or what the speaker believes is the right course of action.

#### Obligation and Necessity
*   **need to**: Used to express what the speaker considers personal, practical importance (not a strict rule/law).
*   **have to**: Used to express objective necessity resulting from an external rule, code, or law.
    *   *Example*: You **need to** pack your bag tonight. You **have to** show your passport at the border.

#### Lack of Obligation (Absence of Necessity)
These phrases mean that an action is **not necessary** (you can do it if you want, but there is no rule/need).
*   `don't have to + infinitive`
*   `don't need to + infinitive`
*   `needn't + infinitive` (Note: **needn't** is a modal and is **never** followed by *to*).
    *   *Example*: You don't have to pay. \| You don't need to hurry. \| You needn't bring an umbrella.

### Grammar: The Passive Voice
*   **Structure**: `subject + verb be + past participle`
*   **Usage**: The passive shifts the focus from the performer of the action (the agent) to the recipient of the action. It is used when:
    *   The agent is **unknown** (*"My bag was taken while I was sunbathing."*).
    *   The agent is **unimportant** (*"My phone was delivered."*).
    *   We want to **emphasize** the object of the active sentence (*"I was asked to sing!"*).

#### Passive Tense Forms
| Passive Tense | Form | Example Sentence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present Simple Passive** | `is/are + past participle` | Millions of photos **are posted** online daily. |
| **Past Simple Passive** | `was/were + past participle` | This picture **was drawn** by my grandfather. |
| **Modal Passive** | `modal + be + past participle` | Tickets **must be booked** in advance. |

*   **The Agent**: If we want to state who performed the action in a passive sentence, we use the preposition **by** (*"I was given presents **by** my friends"*).

| **barbecue** | v | to cook food on a metal rack over an open outdoor fire |

| **boil** | v | to cook food in actively boiling water ($100^\circ\text{C}$) |
| **burn** | v | to cook food excessively, damaging its flavor and coloring |
| **freeze** | v | to preserve food by storing it at freezing temperatures |
| **fry** | v | to cook food in a pan of hot oil or fat |
| **grill** | v | to cook food on a flat metal structure directly over a fire |
| **roast** | v | to cook food (meat/vegetables) with dry oven heat or over a fire |
| **steam** | v | to cook food using the vapor produced by boiling water |

| **taste** | v | to test the flavor of food by placing a small amount in your mouth |

*   *Note*: Cooking verbs can be **transitive** (taking a direct object, e.g., *"fry the onions"*) or **intransitive** (not taking an object, e.g., *"the soup is boiling"*).

### Grammar: Relative Clauses (Defining & Non-Defining)
Relative clauses are introduced by relative pronouns: `who` (people), `which` (things), `that` (people/things), `whose` (belonging to), and `where` (places).

#### Defining Relative Clauses
*   Provide **essential** information identifying the specific noun.
*   **No commas** are used.
*   The pronoun can be **omitted** if it is the object of the relative clause (*"The cakes [which] my brother makes are good."*).
*   The pronoun cannot be omitted if it is the subject of the clause (*"The woman **who** runs the café is friendly."*).
*   **that** can replace *who* or *which*.

#### Non-Defining Relative Clauses
*   Provide **extra, non-essential** details about a noun.
*   **Commas must separate** the clause from the rest of the sentence.
*   The relative pronoun **cannot be omitted**.
*   **that** can **never** be used to introduce a non-defining relative clause (you must use *who* or *which*).
    *   *Example*: My brother, **who** is an excellent cook, has written a book. The recipes, **which** he tested, are easy.

### Grammar: Articles (a/an, the, zero article)
#### 1. Indefinite Article: `a / an`
*   Used with singular countable nouns when mentioned for the **first time** (*"I live in a flat."*).
*   Used when declaring someone's **job, profession, or role** (*"My mum is a doctor."*).

#### 2. Definite Article: `the`
*   Used with any noun when mentioned for the **second time** (*"We found a park. The park had tennis courts."*).

*   Used when referring to a **highly specific thing** (*"The school I go to is near."*).
*   Used with names of **mountain ranges** (*the Andes*), plural **states/countries** (*the USA*, *the UK*), and **deserts, oceans, or rivers** (*the Sahara*, *the Pacific*, *the Amazon*).

#### 3. Zero Article (No Article)
*   Used with plural countables and uncountable nouns when discussing them in a **general** sense (*"Cheese is nice."* \| *"Volleyball is fun."*).
*   Used with names of individual cities, countries, continents, single islands, single mountains, and single lakes (*Rome*, *Europe*, *Sicily*, *Mount Fuji*, *Lake Louise*).

### Reporting Verbs
*   **announce** (v): to state something officially or confidently
*   **demand** (v): to request something firmly, expecting no refusal
*   **explain** (v): to clarify something by giving reasons or details
*   **insist** (v): to state or demand something forcefully
*   **suggest** (v): to offer an idea or plan for consideration

### Grammar: Reported Speech (Statements)
When reporting what someone said using a past-tense reporting verb (*said*, *told*, *explained*), the tenses within the statement backshift:

#### Tense Shifts in Reported Speech
| Direct Speech Tense | Reported Speech Tense | Direct Example | Reported Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present Simple** | **Past Simple** | "I enjoy films." | He said he **enjoyed** films. |
| **Present Continuous** | **Past Continuous** | "She is acting well." | He said she **was acting** well. |
| **Past Simple** | **Past Perfect** | "She studied here." | He said she **had studied** there. |
| **Present Perfect** | **Past Perfect** | "I haven't seen her." | He said he **hadn't seen** her. |
| **Future (will)** | **would** | "She'll be famous." | He said she **would** be famous. |
| **Modal (can)** | **could** | "She can sing well." | He said she **could** sing well. |

*   **No Backshift Modals**: Do not change `could`, `would`, `should`, `might`, `used to`, or the `past perfect`.
*   **Pronoun Shifts**: Pronouns must adjust to match the reporter's context (*"I"* becomes *"he/she"*, etc.).

#### Verb Patterns with Say and Tell
*   **say**: Does **not** take a personal indirect object (*"He said that he was tired."*).
*   **tell**: **Must** be followed by a personal indirect object/pronoun (*"He told **us** that he was tired."*).
*   **insist, suggest, demand**: Can be followed by `that + subject + should + base verb` (*"I suggested that we should watch it again."*).

### Vocabulary: Verbs of Communication

| **warn** | v | to inform someone of potential future danger or trouble |
| **wonder** | v | to express a desire to know or understand something |

*   *Note*: Adverbs of degree such as **fairly**, **pretty**, **quite**, and **reasonably** are used to modify the intensity of communicative descriptions (e.g., *fairly quite*, *reasonably sure*).

### Grammar: Reported Questions
Reported questions do **not** use question word order or auxiliary question structures. They are structured as standard statements.

#### Key Structuring Rules
1.  **Word Order**: Subject + Verb (identical to a positive statement, not a question).
    *   *Direct*: "What is the time?" → *Reported*: She asked me what the time **was** (not *what was the time*).
2.  **No Auxiliaries**: The auxiliary verbs `do`, `does`, and `did` are **omitted**.
    *   *Direct*: "When did you leave?" → *Reported*: He asked when I **had left**.
3.  **Yes/No Questions**: Use **if** or **whether** to introduce the reported clause.
    *   *Direct*: "Are you busy?" → *Reported*: She asked **if** I was busy.
4.  **Wh- Questions**: Keep the original question word (*what*, *when*, *where*, *why*, *which*, *how*).
    *   *Direct*: "Where is it?" → *Reported*: He asked where it was.
5.  **Reporting Verbs**: Introduce reported questions using `ask`, `want to know`, or `wonder`.
    *   *Note*: Only *ask* can be followed directly by a personal noun/pronoun (*"She asked **us** what time it was."*).

### Grammar: Clauses of Contrast, Purpose, and Reason
We use conjunctions to join clauses together, stating contrast, purpose, or reason:

#### 1. Contrast Clauses (although, whereas, while)
*   **although**: Used to join two contrasting facts. Can go in the middle or at the start (*"Although it was hard work, I enjoyed it."*).
*   **whereas**: Used to contrast two opposite ideas. Placed in the middle or start (*"I prefer to wake up early, whereas my friends sleep late."*).
*   **while**: Typically placed at the start of a sentence to show contrast (*"While I don't run often, I know it is healthy."*).

#### 2. Purpose Clauses (so that, in order to)
*   **in order to**: Followed directly by a base verb infinitive (*"I got a job in order to save money."*).
*   **so that**: Followed by a subject/object clause (*"I got a job so that I could save money."*).

#### 3. Reason Clauses (because, as)
*   **because**: Explains the cause or reason (*"I left because it was late."*).
*   **as**: Carries the identical meaning of *because* (*"I walked to work as it was a fine morning."*).

### Phrasal Verbs
*   **believe in**: to have trust in the truth, value, or capability of something
*   **depend on**: to be influenced, determined, or supported by someone/something
*   **go for**: to actively try to achieve a goal
*   **get into**: to succeed in being selected or admitted into an organization/school
*   **join in**: to actively participate in a group activity
*   **keep on**: to persistently continue doing an action
*   **miss out**: to fail to experience something enjoyable that others are doing
*   **work out**: to solve or understand a problem after careful thought

### Grammar: Verbs with Two Objects
Many active verbs can have **two objects**: a **direct object** (usually a thing) and an **indirect object** (usually a person).
Verbs that take two objects include: `bring`, `buy`, `cook`, `choose`, `fetch`, `give`, `lend`, `make`, `offer`, `owe`, `pay`, `send`, `show`, `take`, `teach`, `tell`, `throw`, `write`.

There are two primary ways to order these objects:

#### Pattern A: Direct Object First
If the direct object (the thing) is placed first, you must use the prepositions **to** or **for** before the indirect object (the person).
*   **Structure**: `Subject + Verb + Direct Object + to / for + Indirect Object`
    *   *Example (to)*: I sent **a text** to **my aunt**.
    *   *Example (for)*: I bought **flowers** for **my mum**.

#### Pattern B: Indirect Object First
If the indirect object (the person) is placed first, do **not** use *to* or *for*.
*   **Structure**: `Subject + Verb + Indirect Object + Direct Object`
    *   *Example*: I sent **my aunt** **a text**.
    *   *Example*: I bought **my mum** **flowers**.
